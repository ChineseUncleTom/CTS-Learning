Lv.1 · wire, cable & signal flow

One conductor or many 🧵

A signal is the desired information sent through an AV system — video, audio, data, control. Anything else that gets in is noise and interference.

Anna · the source
Right. I'm going to say something at this end, and you're going to hear it exactly as I said it at that end.
Bold. There are forty metres of cable, a dimmer rack and a fluorescent light between us.
…that's what this whole chapter is about, isn't it.
★ SKILL GET! A wire is a solo. A cable is a band.

A wire contains only one signal conductor — solid or stranded — with insulation around it. A cable contains multiple insulated wires in a protected bundle, possibly with shields, under a jacket. That's the whole distinction, and the exam asks it directly: cable contains multiple conductors.

★ SKILL GET! The path is the signal flow.

Signal types get grouped together so their path through the system is easier to trace, and that path is called the signal flow. There may be separate flows for video, audio and control. Not "signal transfer route" — the exam offers that to see if you'll take it.

Lv.2 · the anatomy of a cable

What a signal wears 🧅

Four layers, from the middle out. Learn them in order once and half this chapter falls into place.

★ SKILL GET! Core, coat, chainmail, cloak.

Conductor (the core that carries the current) → insulation (the coat that stops conductors touching) → shield (the chainmail against interference) → jacket (the cloak against the world). If there's a shield, the jacket goes over it; if not, straight over the insulated wires.

Lv.3 · conductors

Solid or stranded 🪢

A conductor is a material that allows a current to pass through it continuously, carrying the signal between a source (a microphone) and a load (a mixing console). Classified by size (gauge or cross-sectional area), construction, and conductive material.

Digi · the destination
Solid conductor. One strand. Cheaper, stiffer, takes more strain. Obviously correct.
I'm on stage, Digi. Somebody stands on me four times a night. I want stranded — finer strands, more flexible, better flex life.
It costs more and it's slightly fatter for the same cross-section.
I am being stood on.
★ SKILL GET! Solid is stiff and cheap. Stranded is bendy and dear.

Solid — single strand, costs less, less flexible, more restrictive bend radius, but withstands more strain than stranded of similar cross-section. Not suitable where regular flexing is required. Stranded — many small conductors twisted or braided; the finer the strands, the more flexible; more expensive and slightly larger for the same cross-section. Used for patch leads and live events.

📐 two terms worth keeping straight Bend radius = how much you can bend a cable before it deforms and alters its electrical properties. Flex life = how long a wire lasts before it physically fails to conduct properly. One is a limit, the other is a lifespan.
🔥 gauge The cross-sectional area, or gauge, must be big enough to handle the intended signal's current without offering too much electrical resistance or producing too much heat.
Lv.4 · insulation & shields

The coat and the chainmail 🛡

Insulation prevents physical contact between conductors and avoids signal interactions between them. Bare wires touching create short circuits that prevent transmission and damage equipment.

★ SKILL GET! Heat helps the conductor and hurts the insulator.

Straight from the book, and beautifully counterintuitive: most conductors increase in conductivity with temperature, while higher temperatures result in lower dielectric strength in an insulator. And dielectric strength is the ability of an insulating material to block electric fields. Insulation is made from PVC, silicone, rubber or PTFE (Teflon).

★ SKILL GET! Foil needs a drain. Braid is its own drain.

The one line that separates the three shield types. Foil — aluminium foil wrapped round the conductors, needing an additional uninsulated conductor in continuous contact with it to provide the electrical drain or ground. Braided — fine uninsulated wires interwoven into a cover, sometimes in multiple layers, where ground termination is made directly with the braid. Combination — both, possibly plus a drain wire.

choosing a shield

Coverage · flexibility · frequency

  • Coverage — expressed as a percentage, how much of the inner cable is covered
  • Flexibility — subjective, and directly correlates to flex life
  • Frequency range — no shield stops all EM interference, so its effectiveness is narrowed to a certain band
it works both ways

Shields protect the room too 🛡

A shield doesn't only keep interference out. It's also used to protect other devices and cables from the EM radiation generated by the signals in the enclosed circuit.

Your noisy cable is somebody else's interference.

The exam's favourite one-liner: the purpose of shielding is to prevent noise from mixing with the signal. Not insulation, not jackets, not conductors. Noise.

Jackets — the cloak 🧥

the job

Physical protection

Jackets (also called sheaths) surround and protect the inner wires and insulate the conductors from environmental factors. Chosen for strength, integrity, abrasion resistance and overall protection.

Also weighed: UV radiation, corrosive vapours, colour (aesthetic, identity, labelling), flexibility, flammability, emissions on combustion, and electrical code requirements.

materials

Teflon vs silicone

Mostly the same materials as insulation. Some plastics are unsuitable in certain locations because of flammability or the toxic chemicals they produce in a fire.

PTFE (Teflon) — withstands high heat, won't support a fire, very little smoke. But hard to work with and more expensive.

Silicone — flexible, robust, easy to work with, high temperatures.

Lv.5 · the three cable types

Coax, twisted-pair, fibre 🎛

Three types the book says you should be familiar with — and you already met two of them in Chapter 6.

★ SKILL GET! Seventy-five for the eyes. Fifty for the sky.

The two coax impedances, and they are not interchangeable. 75 Ω = video (the eyes). 50 Ω = data and radio frequency (the sky). Coax is unbalanced: the single centre wire is the signal conductor and the shield acts as the return. The name comes from the shield and centre conductor sharing the same central axis.

coax

One conductor, unbalanced

A single conductor surrounded by insulation, then one or two shields — the first foil or braid, the second usually braided — then a jacket.

Its impedance is mostly set by the capacitive coupling between the central conductor and the shield, which is strongly influenced by the dielectric strength of the insulation.

twisted-pair

Pairs that cancel

Pairs of insulated wires twisted around each other. Pairs may be individually shielded to avoid crosstalk with other pairs, and the whole cable may have a shield for external interference. Spacing materials between pairs reduce crosstalk further.

Twisting, combined with balanced circuitry, helps reject common mode interference — the exam wants the words rejecting interference.

fibre

Light, not current 💡

Conductors of transparent glass or plastic, so visible light or infrared signals travel instead of a current.

Single-mode = one light path, yellow jacket, reaches farther without attenuation. Multimode = multiple paths, orange jacket. Same as Chapter 6 — yellow goes far, orange stays near.

Anna · still on stage
So the twist itself does the cancelling?
The twist plus balanced circuitry. Same trick as Chapter 4 — two wires that disagree cancel the noise. Common mode rejection. 🐾
Lv.6 · connectors

The ends of everything 🔌

Connectors are terminated onto cables and used on equipment to continue the pathway to the electronics inside. The connector on the cable must be compatible with the one on the equipment.

🤝 five ways a connector mates Twisting · twist and locking · threading · screw or snap-down lock · contact pressure. That's the book's full list of mating categories.
🎛 gadget 02 · the connector drawer 15 connectors

Filter the drawer by what the connector actually carries.

★ SKILL GET! Count the rows: HD15 has three, DB15 has two.

HD15 (VGA) is sometimes incorrectly called a DB15. They are not the same: DB15 has two rows of pins, HD15 has three. HD15 is found on computers and monitors using analog video.

★ SKILL GET! D is digital, A is analog, DL is dual, I is integrated.

DVI carries digital and analog video plus control through a 29-pin connector, and omitting pins makes the variants: DVI-D digital only · DVI-A VGA-compatible analog only · DVI-DL dual digital streams · DVI-I the full integrated system of digital, analog and control.

Lv.7 · signal integrity

Keeping Anna intact ✨

The goal is simple: preserve the signal's integrity until it arrives at its destination. Two enemies live inside the cable itself, and several more live in the room.

enemy 1

Capacitance

Occurs when two conductors are separated by a nonconductive material — the electrostatic field between them stores some charge.

How much is governed by the area of the conductors, the distance between them, and the dielectric strength of the material separating them.

Capacitance opposes changes of voltage. The higher the frequency, the lower the capacitive losses — and significant capacitance attenuates and distorts the signal.

enemy 2

Crosstalk

Each wire in a cable can act as an antenna, collecting signals from the other wires around it. That's crosstalk — the reason twisted pairs get shielded from each other, not just from the outside world.

★ SKILL GET! Keep your own kind together.

Group cables by signal type and then physically separate the groups. Mic-level cables bundle only with mic-level; line-level only with line-level. Putting cables of different voltages and frequencies near each other creates an environment where the signals mutually interfere.

🎛 gadget 03 · route Anna's cable clean

Purple is what Anna sent. Mint is what reaches Digi.

⚡ what to run away from Avoid running cables near motors, fluorescent or dimmed lighting, power distribution cables and electrical power control systems. Also battery charging and radio transmitter systems.
📉 three faces of degradation Signal degradation shows up as voltage amplitude reductions, changes in the shape of the waveform, or phase shifting. And analog systems are much more susceptible to noise and distortion than digital — which is why the best defence is proper system design and installation using industry best practices and established performance standards.
Lv.8 · distance limits

The twist in the tale 📏

A signal transmitted at the proper level will lose strength or amplitude over distance. Three things decide how far it gets.

★ SKILL GET! Digital is tougher but shorter.

Here's the one that catches people, because Chapter 3 spent a whole chapter making digital the hero. It still is — digital resists noise far better. But digital signals generally do not travel as far as analog signals without buffering or amplification. Tougher, and shorter.

Digi · wounded
Hold on. In Chapter 3 I was immune to noise. A smudged 7 is still a 7!
You still are. You just don't get as far before somebody has to buffer or amplify you. Immunity isn't stamina 💅
…noted.
📐 the three factors How far a signal travels is set by signal level, signal bandwidth, and the loss factors of the cable type used. Every signal type has unique characteristics, so consult published standards and the cable manufacturer's documentation for limits by signal type and frequency. Each type may also need a unique cable configuration when splitting or looping within a system.
Lv.9 · switchers

Pick one, not several 🔀

A switcher allows users to select among two or more sources and send the selected signal to a particular destination.

★ SKILL GET! A switcher picks one. A mixer blends many.

The distinction the exam is built on. Switchers send only one of their multiple inputs to their output — as opposed to mixers, which send a combination of several sources to a destination simultaneously. Switchers may be passive (no power) or active (adjustable amplification, equalization and buffering on each input so switching doesn't produce disturbances on the output).

★ SKILL GET! Ins first, outs second.

A matrix switcher can send any of its inputs to one or more of its outputs simultaneously. It's a composite of several switchers — one per output. So a 4 × 2 contains two four-input switchers, and can connect any of four inputs to one or both of two outputs. To avoid loading at the inputs, each input contains a distribution amplifier.

★ SKILL GET! Mechanical crashes. Seamless waits for the gap.

A mechanical switcher makes a physical connection like a wall switch, and pays no attention to a signal's content. Fine for analog audio; a major issue for video or network data that come in discrete frames or packets — the picture jumps, rolls or breaks up because the switch happened mid-frame. That's why they're called crash switchers. Seamless switchers use electronic circuitry to monitor the sync information, buffer the video, and make the transition during the interval between frames.

Chapter 5 called this vertical interval switching versus crash switching. Same idea, same words — switch in the gap, or you crash. Two chapters, one rule ✧

Lv.10 · distribution amplifiers

The amplifier that won't amplify 🌱

★ SKILL GET! A DA is an amplifier with a gain of one.

Word for word from the book. It does not increase signal levels. Instead it allows a single input signal to be distributed to multiple outputs while maintaining the original signal level and quality. Gain of one is unity gain — the same term Chapter 4 gave you for "passing through unchanged".

Anna · confused
An amplifier… that doesn't amplify?
It copies. One of you in, twenty-four of you out, all at the same volume you started at.

video DAs

Every format

Available for all formats of analog and digital video, in balanced and unbalanced versions, ranging from as few as two to more than a dozen outputs. They may also have audio distribution channels for simultaneous video and audio.

the classic use

The press feed 🎤

A speech-reinforcement system needing up to 24 media feeds. The speaker uses one microphone at a lectern rather than being hidden behind two dozen of them.

The media plug into a socket giving a fixed, isolated signal from a DA output — so if one journalist has a shorted cable, it affects only that one feed.

Lv.11 · rack-building considerations

Nineteen inches of home 🗄

An AV rack is a housing unit that protects and organizes electronic equipment — that exact phrase is a question.

★ SKILL GET! Nineteen inside. Twenty-one to twenty-five outside.

The measurement question, and all four options are plausible numbers. Inside: 19 inches (482.6 mm). Outside: 533 to 635 mm (21 to 25 inches). Height runs from about 300 mm (1 foot) to more than 2.1 m (7 feet).

★ SKILL GET! A rack unit is one and three quarters.

1 RU = 44.45 mm = 1.75 inches of usable mounting space. So a 300 mm rack is 6RU and a 2.1 m rack is 48RU. Equipment mounting points are an industry-standard 18.312 inches (465 mm) centre to centre. A mixer might be 1RU, an amplifier 2RU, a switcher 10RU or taller.

two diagrams

Elevation & separation

A rack elevation diagram shows the front and back of a rack and the number of RUs required for each piece of gear.

A signal separation diagram depicts the back of a rack and the cables connecting each piece of equipment.

Planning considers how much heat a piece emits, the types of signals carried to it, and whether it has an interface component for a typical user.

heat

Cool in the bottom, warm out the top 🔥

All electronic devices produce waste heat that builds up and must be removed. Installers use RUs to calculate vertical spacing for ventilation and airflow paths at the rear.

Many devices vent from the top and bottom of the chassis — and hot air leaving one piece may flow into the air intakes of another and eventually cause equipment failure. Vents direct cool air in from the bottom, forcing warm air out through the top.

balance & reach

Heavy low, controls handy

Where racks are freestanding and subject to possible overbalancing, install the heaviest equipment at the bottom to keep the centre of gravity as low as practicable.

Equipment with user interface components — removable media, user-adjustable controls — goes within convenient reach of users, and there may be regulations in your location governing placement.

The cheat sheet 📋 ✧

Three blocks, twelve lines.

🧵 what a cable is

  1. A wire is a solo, a cable is a band. Wire = one conductor; cable = multiple conductors. The traced path of a signal type is the signal flow.
  2. Core, coat, chainmail, cloak — conductor, insulation, shield, jacket. Shielding prevents NOISE from mixing with the signal.
  3. Solid is stiff and cheap. Stranded is bendy and dear (and slightly fatter for the same cross-section). Bend radius = a limit; flex life = a lifespan.
  4. Heat helps the conductor and hurts the insulator. Dielectric strength = ability to block electric fields.
  5. Foil needs a drain. Braid is its own drain. Combination = both. Choose on coverage (a %), flexibility, frequency range — and shields protect the room from your cable too.
  6. PTFE (Teflon) = high heat, won't support fire, little smoke, but pricey and hard to work. Silicone = flexible, robust, easy, high temperature.

🎛 the three types & keeping them clean

  1. Seventy-five for the eyes, fifty for the sky. Coax is unbalanced (shield = return), 75 Ω video / 50 Ω data & RF, not interchangeable. Twisted-pair + balanced = rejecting interference. Fibre: yellow single-mode goes far, orange multimode stays near.
  2. Count the rows: HD15 three, DB15 two. DVI: Digital · Analog · Dual Link · Integrated, on a 29-pin connector. Speakon NL4 = 4 poles; captive screw = Euroblock/Phoenix.
  3. Keep your own kind together — bundle mic with mic, line with line, and separate the groups. Capacitance opposes voltage change (higher frequency → lower capacitive loss); crosstalk is every wire acting as an antenna. Avoid motors, dimmers, fluorescents, power cabling.
  4. Digital is tougher but shorter — it won't travel as far as analog without buffering or amplification. Distance depends on signal level, bandwidth, and the cable's loss factors.

🔀 boxes & racks

  1. A switcher picks one, a mixer blends many. Ins first, outs second — a 4 × 2 sends any of four inputs to one or both of two outputs, contains two switchers, and has a DA on every input. Mechanical crashes, seamless waits for the gap. A DA is an amplifier with a gain of one.
  2. Nineteen inside, twenty-one to twenty-five outside, one and three quarters per U (1RU = 44.45 mm; mounting points 18.312 in / 465 mm centre to centre). A rack protects and organizes. Heaviest at the bottom; cool air in the bottom, warm out the top. Rack elevation = front/back with RUs; signal separation = back with cables.
