Neko Notes
Kit

The house style for CTS study notes. Chibi café, sticker outlines, screentone dots, and two cats who disagree about everything. Copy the token block, use the thirteen parts, and every chapter will look like it came from the same notebook ✧

why it looks like this

Five rules holding it together (๑˃ᴗ˂)ﻭ

Cute, but the facts stay exact

The jokes and kaomoji are the sugar. Every number, definition and list is quoted straight from the textbook, unchanged. Nothing gets softened for the sake of a gag.

Facts get set in mono

Numbers and specs live in --f-mono inside dark .stats panels. Explanations are in the round body face. Cat dialogue and margin notes are in the handwriting face. You can find the examinable bits by squinting.

Colour carries meaning

Lavender is always analog. Mint is always digital or correct. Berry is wrong or lossy. Sun is emphasis and never a category. Never recolour a part for variety.

Everything is a sticker

3px outline, hard offset shadow, no soft blur anywhere. If a thing has no outline, it's not a component — it's a mistake.

No walls of prose

Any passage over ~60 words gets the .story treatment: one beat per idea, an emoji waypoint in the gutter, a highlight on the sentence that answers the question, and a figure in the rail beside it. A dense paragraph reads as "nothing here" and gets skipped.

One SKILL GET per concept

The gold card appears at most once per section and holds a single memorable line. Two mnemonics for one idea is zero mnemonics.

🌙 about dark mode Dark isn't an inversion — it's the café at midnight. The ground goes plum-brown rather than grey, and every pastel brightens so it still pops against it. The outline goes darker (#0F0A12), because a sticker outline must always be the darkest thing on the card.
🔤 text on a coloured chip Never put white on a saturated accent — white on berry measures only 3.8 : 1 in light mode and fails AA. Use --tag-ink (#2A0E14), the one ink that clears 4.5 : 1 on berry in both themes. And never colour a chip with --line on --paper text: in dark mode both are dark, so the label vanishes. Level tags use --sun + --line.
typography

Four voices ✎

All four are system stacks — nothing loads over the network, so nothing silently falls back to Times.

body · explanations
Digi can't hear humming — he can only write numbers on a ticket. So he takes a snapshot of how loud Anna is, over and over.

Trebuchet is the roundest readable system sans. 17px, line-height 1.7, measure capped at 62 characters.

--f-body: "Trebuchet MS", "Segoe UI", system-ui, -apple-system, Verdana, sans-serif

.cat-card + the sprites

The mascots. Same chibi silhouette drawn twice: one in pure curves, one in pure right angles — right down to the blush (ellipse vs rectangle) and the mouth. That contrast is the analog/digital lesson, which is why the cats aren't decoration.

Both live in a hidden <svg><defs> block and are placed with <use href="#anna">, so one edit updates every appearance on the page.

Reuse them across chapters as the two poles of any either/or — they're already colour-coded in the reader's head.

Nnnh, I contain infinite values between off and on… (´｡• ᵕ •｡`)
I contain two. One is on. Zero is off. I sleep very well.

.say / .bubble

Cat dialogue. The tail is two stacked CSS triangles — the outer one is the outline, the inner one fills. Add .right to flip the cat to the other side and mirror the tail.

Use to voice the intuition before the formal definition, or to nudge the reader ("sing it three times now").

Always wrap a kaomoji in .kmj — they contain literal spaces, so a face like (´｡• ᵕ •｡`) will otherwise break across two lines and read as garbage. .kmj is just white-space:nowrap; heading faces get it via .kao.

Not for facts. Nothing examinable should exist only inside a bubble.

.scene — the 8-bit hero

A pixel-art café drawn on a canvas that is only 124 logical pixels tall, then blown up by CSS with image-rendering:pixelated. Every pixel on screen is a real pixel — nothing is faked with a filter.

Animated: clouds drift, birds flap across, steam curls off the chimney, the windows flicker, and the OPEN sign pulses. Day and night are two full palettes, swapped by theme.

Use as the header of every chapter — it is the one place atmosphere beats information.

Not behind body text. Headline type only, and only with the outlined h1 treatment.

★ SKILL GET! Double the top, or the tune gets chopped.

Nyquist–Shannon: sample at more than twice the highest frequency present. 20 kHz hearing → more than 40 kHz → the CD's 44.1 kHz.

.skill — the menu board

The mnemonic, served on the café's menu board: a wooden frame, a wiped writing surface with faint ruled lines, and a marker tray along the bottom rail with a pink and a mint marker in it. The berry tab notches into the top edge; the line itself sits in Japanese corner brackets 「 」.

The board is a plain --paper surface on purpose — the mnemonic is the single most re-read sentence in the chapter, so nothing patterned may sit behind that text. Decoration stays on the frame and the tray, outside the reading area.

Use once per section, right after the concept is introduced.

Never two in a row — they cancel each other out.

Variant: swap the background to --lav-soft and the tab to ♪ SONG GET! for a chant or song — the frame recolours, the board stays readable.

⚠ exam trap #2 Bit depth is the number of STATES — not signals, not speeds, not rates. Digi only knows how to stack states.

.note

The sticky note, taped on with a strip of mint washi and tilted half a degree. Handwriting face. Holds jokes, exam traps, nerd trivia.

Use for anything a reader in a hurry could skip and lose nothing.

Label it with .note-h: ⚠ exam trap, 🎬 nerd corner, 🔢 the number trick.

.story — the long-paragraph pattern

Never ship a wall of prose. Two dense paragraphs read as "nothing here" and get skipped, no matter how good the writing is. Any passage over roughly 60 words gets broken into this shape:

1 · One beat per idea. Each .beat is a short paragraph with an emoji in the gutter. The emoji is a waypoint — the eye lands on it and knows a new idea started.

2 · A .story-lead on top naming what the passage is about, so a skimmer gets the point without reading the body.

3 · A figure in the rail, sitting beside the prose it explains. Draw the actual mechanism, not decoration — these two redraw the textbook's own Figures 3-5 and 3-6.

Cap it at three or four beats. More than that isn't a story, it's a list — use .cat-list or a table instead.

The clerk reads it and types a clean new page, so the hundredth copy is identical to the first — whereas the portrait is the smudges.

.hl / .hl-p / .hl-m — the highlighter

A marker swipe that sits behind the text as a gradient band from 56% to 94% height, so it never touches the letterforms and the ink colour never changes. In dark mode the band goes dark and the light text still reads.

Sun for the sentence that answers the question. Mint for the digital upside. Pink for the one phrase you want quoted back in the exam.

At most two per paragraph. Highlighting everything highlights nothing — and don't stack it on top of <b>; pick one.

.stats

The RPG stat block — a dark panel with a honey header and dotted leader lines. Any set of numbers that belongs together: a calculation, a spec sheet, two positions compared.

Use for numbers you must memorise. The .tot row is the punchline.

Not for prose. If it needs a full sentence, it isn't a stat block.

🎛 gadget 02 · grayscale ramp 16 states
4-bit

4-bit is the classic 16-colour display — and the 16-shade grey ramp in your book.

.gadget

The interactive instrument, and the most important part of the system. A title, a live .verdict pill, a screen, one control, and a handwritten reaction line that changes with the value.

Every chapter needs at least one thing the reader can push until it goes wrong. A slider that visibly breaks the wave below 2× teaches Nyquist in four seconds.

👾 THE EXAM SLIME
HP 4 / 7 — it stopped smiling.

.boss + .opt

The chapter's own review questions, reframed as a boss fight. Each correct answer takes 1 HP off the slime; the bar goes mint → sun → berry as it drops, and the taunt line under it changes every hit.

Options are buttons, not radios — one click reveals and locks. Correct goes mint, chosen-wrong goes berry, and the right answer lights up beside it so a wrong guess still teaches.

The bar is position:sticky inside the quiz section, so it floats at the top of the screen for the whole fight — you always see the damage you're doing. A zero-height sentinel above it drives a .stuck class that shrinks it from 138px to 70px once it lifts off, so a floating bar never eats the screen.

Never use mint or berry decoratively — the reader has learned they mean right and wrong.

  3. Snaps × Shades × Speakers.

.sheet

Closes every chapter: the whole thing compressed into one numbered list of the mnemonics, in the order they appeared. It's the only place numbering is allowed, because it's the only place the order is a real sequence to recite.

Cap it at 12 lines. If it runs longer, the chapter has too many hooks.

.topbar / .chapnav — site chrome

Back-to-home plus previous/next chapter, in two places: a sticky top bar that stays put through a very long page, and a footer pair at the point where you've finished reading and actually want to move on.

These are not authored into the pages. They're injected by tools/build.mjs from the site's chapter list, so adding a chapter updates the nav on every other page by itself — and the Artifact copies of the pages stay standalone.

First and last chapters get a dashed, non-clickable tile rather than a missing one, so the row never collapses and you can see you're at an end.

Watch the sticky stack. The top bar and the boss HP bar are both sticky, so the boss offset is --navh + --sp-2. Add a third sticky element and you must budget for it too.
